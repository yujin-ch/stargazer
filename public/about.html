About Stargazer

A daily one-minute picture-naming game. Look through the telescope and name what drifts by.

Stargazer is a small, free browser game. Every night there is one new sky, and everyone in the world gets the same one. You have sixty seconds at the eyepiece. Objects drift into view one after another (a teapot, a lighthouse, a pangolin) and you type what you see. Name it and the next one arrives. There is no time to second-guess, which is the fun of it.

The game is made to be cozy and a little mysterious: a telescope on a dark hill, a lantern, a turning brass focus ring, and a sky that shifts colour with your local hour. It plays on phones and computers, needs no account, and takes about a minute a day.

How to play

  • Type what you see. You don't need to press Enter: a right answer is accepted as soon as it's typed, and the telescope moves on by itself. Common names and spellings count. If you're one letter off, it asks "did you mean …?" and Enter takes it.
  • Name it while it's zoomed in for a bonus. Each object starts close and blurry, then pulls back into focus. The sooner you recognise it, the more it's worth.
  • Skip anything you can't place (Tab on a keyboard). A skip costs two seconds, and every right answer gives a second or more back (more for harder finds).
  • Answers in a row multiply your points: ×1.1 after three, ×1.25 after six and ×1.5 after ten.
  • One sky a night. When the clock runs out you see your observing log, how you compare with everyone on tonight's hill, and a picture of your night to share.

The five kinds of find

Every object belongs to one of five difficulty bands. Harder finds are rarer, start more zoomed in and blurrier, and are worth far more.

  • Moon
    everyday things · 10 points
  • Planet
    a little trickier · 20 points
  • Comet
    takes a second look · 30 points
  • Nebula
    hard to place · 50 points
  • Black Hole
    very rare · 80 points
  • ???
    some nights, something else is out there

A zoomed-in answer is worth half again as much. Name everything before time is up and you get five points for every second left on the clock.

Your night, drawn as stars

After each run Stargazer draws your night as a star dial. Around the dial is the minute, starting at the top, and further out is harder. Answers in a row join into a constellation, which is named after your best find. No two nights look the same, and the picture never gives away an answer, so you can share it freely.

Questions

Is it free?
Yes. Stargazer is free to play. It may show an advertisement on the results screen, never while you're playing.
When does the new sky arrive?
At midnight in your own time zone. The results screen counts down to it as the moon rises over the hill.
Can I play yesterday's sky?
Yes. Open the Star Atlas and look under Past skies. Earlier nights can be played any time, they just don't count toward your log.
Do I need an account?
No. Your log, streak and universe live in your own browser. If you like, you can sign in (with Google or an email link) so they follow you to other devices.
Where do the pictures come from?
Each object is an illustration generated for the game, then cut out and pixelated to look like it's seen through an old telescope.
Something looks wrong, or I have an idea.
We'd love to hear it: hello@playstargazer.io.
